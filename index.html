How I make decisions

Six questions I use to turn an AI idea into decisions that operations and engineering can act on.

Patient scheduling

What should the assistant handle?

Start with a request to change an appointment. Choose a lens to see how it changes the design.

Back to the scheduling case

Where is the friction?

The question I’d ask
Can patients change an appointment without repeating the same information or starting over after a transfer?
How it shapes the work
Map the rescheduling journey first. Define what information the assistant needs and what should carry through if staff take over.

What will staff actually use?

The question I’d ask
Can scheduling staff act on the handoff while handling a live queue?
How it shapes the work
Review a sample handoff with staff and ask what they need to pick up the call without restarting the conversation.

When should the assistant stop?

The question I’d ask
What happens when the assistant cannot verify the caller or complete the change?
How it shapes the work
Withhold appointment details when verification fails and route the caller to staff. Confirm a change only after the scheduling system records it.

What changes across systems?

The question I’d ask
Do the scheduling platforms support the same lookup, availability, and rescheduling actions?
How it shapes the work
Validate each platform’s capabilities before promising a shared experience. Define a staff handoff for actions the system cannot complete.

What should the caller know?

The question I’d ask
Does the caller understand whether the appointment was actually changed?
How it shapes the work
Confirm the new date and time after the change is recorded. If the update fails, tell the caller what happened and how to get help.

How would I know it helped?

The question I’d ask
Are more callers completing their request with less effort?
How it shapes the work
Track completed reschedules, staff transfers, and repeat calls together. Check whether the experience improved for callers and staff.

Enterprise AI Readiness Framework™

Seven areas I assess to decide what’s ready, what needs work first, and how to move forward.

Executive alignment

What I look for
Is there a sponsor who can make decisions about scope, budget, and ownership?
What I’d do with the finding
Agree on the operating goal and name the executive who owns it.

Workflow readiness

What I look for
Can the team explain how the work happens today, including exceptions and handoffs?
What I’d do with the finding
Map the current workflow with staff and agree which steps are ready for automation.

Data accessibility

What I look for
Can the system reliably access the data it needs and record the actions it takes?
What I’d do with the finding
Verify access, data quality, and integration limits before committing to scope.

AI governance

What I look for
What information can the assistant use or disclose, and when must it stop?
What I’d do with the finding
Document privacy controls, escalation rules, and who signs off before launch.

Human adoption

What I look for
Do the people who use or support the workflow know how their work will change?
What I’d do with the finding
Test the workflow with frontline staff and agree who owns training and handoffs.

Operationalization

What I look for
Can the team test, launch, and support the workflow in production?
What I’d do with the finding
Define launch criteria, assign signoff owners, and agree on how to pause or roll back.

Continuous optimization

What I look for
Will the team see where the workflow succeeds, fails, or creates more work?
What I’d do with the finding
Set a review cadence and use results, staff feedback, and exceptions to prioritize improvements.
